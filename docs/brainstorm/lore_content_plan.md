# Lore Content Expansion — Implementation Plan

Status (2026-11-07): **planned, not started.** Builds on the generated-lore
base (steps 1–5 of [lore_plan.md](lore_plan.md)), which is on branch
`claude/lore-history-4d1fe2` at `b33a8dc` and **not yet on `dev`**. Every file,
function and number below was read or measured on that branch. All draft text
in this plan and in the data it proposes is placeholder; the owner rewrites it
(see "Where the owner rewrites text").

## Decision

The base works: every site has a history, a chapter picks its scenario, and
evidence stands where it happened. The owner's direction (2026-11-07) is to
make it richer and less often empty:

- **Fewer completely quiet worlds.** Most sites carry *something*: a hostile
  pocket, a hazard or a relic with a sting. Not all of them are hard.
  Chapters gain a **tier**: `threat` (today's scenarios), `hazard` (new: a
  small, local, finite danger layered on the map's calm base scenario) and
  `quiet`. Target per map: **threat 35%, hazard 45%, quiet 20%**.
- **More happens per site.** Roughly double the events per site (≈4 today),
  more of them with a place on the map, more than one civilisation per site
  over time, and older ruins under newer ones.
- **Civilisations have a tech level**, primitive to spacefaring, which gates
  what they build and what they leave. Pool civs (the ones that travel) are
  spacefaring; native civs start low and may climb.
- **Planet-bound civilisations can destroy each other.** A two-civ war arc
  where neither reaches space, with forts on both sides, a battlefield
  between, graves, and each side's own version of the war on its datapads.
  Variants: one side survives as a remnant, or a spacefaring visitor uplifts
  or enslaves a primitive people.
- **Structures are procedural and purposeful.** Ships of several classes and
  sizes, and ground structures by tech level, generated by parameterised
  structure scripts that take what the event knows (size, purpose, damage,
  civ, tech, what it holds) and leave clues to why the place was built.
- **Density never costs playability.** A per-site budget for stamps and
  spawns, a protected landing zone, and a validation test that sweeps seeds.

Decided constraints carried over: all history is alien; scenarios stay random
from the player's view (no picker); the enemy
factions plan's presences layer on top of history and are not part of this
plan; start systems (first jumps) are always safe.

## Design

### 1. Hazard tiers instead of quiet

#### Tiers on chapters

`EventDef` gains `Tier string` (`"threat"`, `"hazard"`). A chapter is an
event with a scenario **or** a hazard tier:

```go
// Tier is the chapter's weight class: "threat" (a full scenario; the default
// for an event with a Scenario) or "hazard" (a local, finite danger that
// plays the map's base_scenario with the event's evidence on top).
Tier string `json:"tier,omitempty"`

func (e *EventDef) IsChapter() bool { return e.Scenario != "" || e.Tier == TierHazard }
func (e *EventDef) ChapterTier() string // "threat" if Scenario != "" && Tier == ""
```

A hazard chapter usually has no `scenario`; `commit` sets
`site.Scenario = md.BaseScenario` for it, exactly as for quiet.
`SiteHistory` gains `Tier string` (`quiet`/`hazard`/`threat`), saved; old
saves derive it on load from the chapter's def (no version bump).

A hazard may also name a scenario when the scenario is gentle: `scout_accident`
(crashed_ufo with a dead crew) becomes `"tier": "hazard"`.

#### Tier roll replaces quiet_weight

`Present` gains:

```json
"tiers": { "threat": 35, "hazard": 45, "quiet": 20 },
"safe_tiers": { "hazard": 60, "quiet": 40 }
```

`chooseChapter` becomes two rolls on the site stream:

1. Roll a tier by `tiers` (`safe_tiers` on a first jump).
2. Among that tier's eligible chapters (same eligibility and trial clones as
   today, minus `repeats`), pick by per-map `weight`. Within a tier the
   weights are relative, so today's calibrated threat weights keep their
   equal-scenario shares among threats.
3. Nothing eligible in the tier: fall back threat → hazard → quiet, and
   hazard → quiet. Quiet always succeeds.

`quiet_weight` stays readable for one release as a fallback (no `tiers` block
→ old behaviour) so the step can land map by map; `TestLoreMapsHavePresents`
then requires `tiers` on every history map. `quiet_safe` is replaced by
`safe_tiers` (quiet is allowed on a first jump iff `safe_tiers.quiet > 0`).

**Safe-start rule.** A first jump never rolls `threat`. It rolls `hazard` or
`quiet` by `safe_tiers`, and within `hazard` only chapters with
`safe_start: true`. Hazards default to `safe_start: false`; the inert ones
(radiation, blight, worm sign, toxic flora, unstable ground, scout_accident)
set it true. `BindSiteSafe` keeps its name and signature.

#### Calm base scenarios

For 20% "truly quiet" to mean quiet, four maps need a calm `base_scenario`:

| Map | Base today | New base | Today's base becomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| desert_planet | `worms` | `desert_calm` (new: no spawn rules) | `burrowers_stir` gains a desert weight (threat) |
| frozen_planet | `yeti_territory` | `frozen_calm` (new: critters on snow) | `yeti_return` (threat) and `hostile_fauna` (hazard) |
| alien, ocean_planet | `alien_world` | `alien_wilds` (new: critters + `alien_flora`) | `reactor_mutation`, `wreck_reactor_leak` (threat), `mutant_band` (hazard) |
| abandoned_station | `abandoned_station` | `abandoned_station` with its mutant spawn rule removed | `station_experiment` (threat) keeps the mutants |

The three new scenarios are small JSON files with setup scripts copied from
the current ones minus hostiles. `TestLoreScenariosCoveredAndSupported` keeps
`worms`, `yeti_territory` and `alien_world` reachable as threats.

#### Structure guards stop being free hostiles

Every structure script gets an `occupants` param (§5). Default `"guards"`
keeps today's mutant brute and grunts, so quest fixtures and scenario stamps
are unchanged. History evidence passes it explicitly: `"none"` for ruins on
quiet sites, the hazard's own choice on hazard sites, `"guards"` only where
the chronicle says someone lairs there. Without this, density (§2) would turn
every site into a mutant fortress and the tier shares would be fiction.

#### The hazard library

Each hazard is an event in `events.json` with `"tier": "hazard"`, an origin
(often `origin_from` an earlier event, so the hazard grows out of the
history), its evidence anchored at that origin, a `keep_out` (minimum
distance from the landing zone, §6), an `omen`, and lore flags
(`hazard: <id>`). Spawns are **finite and placed at generation**: no
continuous spawn rules. Hostile count per hazard is at most 6.

Anchored areal kinds must not be area-scaled: anchored features (`At != ""`)
skip the area multiplier in `generation`, so "2 eggs" means 2 on every map
size.

#### Telling the tiers apart on the Star Map

`Location` gains `Tier` (set in `bindHistory` from `site.Tier`, saved). The
Star Map info panel draws a one-word tag before the summary, coloured:
**Calm** (blue-grey), **Hazard** (amber), **Threat** (red). The omen line
already says what; the tag says how bad. It shows from charting, like the
summary. This tells the player the danger class, not the scenario, so
scenarios stay random from the player's view and nobody picks. The vmtest
`lore_site` grows a check that the tag matches `site.Tier`.

Origins are capped by the stamp budget (§6), not by events: an event past the
budget still gets its datapad (a lore datapad needs no stamp).

#### Generator changes (`internal/history`)

1. **Repeating stages.** `StageDef.Repeat []int` (`[min, max]`): the stage
   picks that many times, each pick re-checked for eligibility and for
   same-type repeats on the site (an event type repeats only if it sets
   `repeatable: true`, for battles and interludes). `walk` loops.
2. **Optional odds.** `StageDef.Chance int` (default 50) replaces the
   hard-coded `rng.Intn(2)` for optional stages, so density can be tuned in
   data.
3. **Interludes.** Events with `"interlude": true` are small, mostly
   origin-less happenings (a figure born or dies, a festival, a migration, a
   raid, a visitor trading, a famine year, a monument raised). After each
   stage, `walk` rolls `Present.Density` (percent, per map, e.g. earth 60,
   asteroid 25) for one eligible interlude. Interludes respect `requires`, so
   they only happen while someone lives there. They leave no stamp; at most
   one in three takes an origin and a datapad.
4. **Layers.** `Present.Layers {chance, arcs}`: with that chance a site first
   runs a short **layer arc** (`"layer": true`, 3–4 stages, ends with its
   resident gone), then its main arc. A main-arc stage marked `"epoch": true`
   (biosphere events) is skipped when its state already holds. New events
   `civ_rises_on_ruins` (native) and `settles_on_ruins` (arrive) need
   `remains: ruins` and `resident: false` instead of `population: never`,
   and fill `{former}` (the layer civ's name), so the chronicle says "the
   Vorr built over the halls of the Ulrani". A native rising there may take
   the former civ as `Ancestor` (30%). Layer chance: earth 40, frozen 35,
   alien 35, ocean 30, desert 25, moon 15, asteroid 10, station 0.
5. **Succession on one site.** Arcs gain an optional "newcomers" stage after
   a decline (`civ_settles|refugees_arrive|settles_on_ruins`), so a site can
   have a second resident without a full layer.
6. **Gaps.** `defaultGapDays` (5–40 years) stays; interludes take
   `gap_days` 1–15 years so a denser chronicle doesn't stretch to millennia.
   Target total span: under 600 years without a layer, under 2,500 with.

Recurrence: keep `civs.json` `reuse` 3. Density raises the number of
`visitor`/`arrive` casts per site, so pool civs recur more without new rules.
Measured target: among pool civs that appear in an 8-site charted campaign,
≥60% appear on 2+ sites (the dump's by-civ section shows this today but
doesn't count it).

Readability: the Legends tab shows only known events, so density reaches the
player only as they discover it. In the chronicle, no two consecutive events
on a site share a type unless `repeatable`; interludes render as one short
sentence. `cmd/history-dump` gains `-stats`, printing per map and per
population: events/site (mean, p90, max), origins/site, stamps/site, tier
shares, civs/site, recurrence, span in years. It is the measuring stick for
every step.

### 3. Civilisation tech levels

#### Model

```go
// Tech levels, in order. Pool civs are spacefaring; natives start low.
const (
    TechPrimitive  = "primitive"
    TechMedieval   = "medieval"
    TechIndustrial = "industrial"
    TechAtomic     = "atomic"
    TechSpacefaring = "spacefaring"
)
```

- `Civ.Tech` is the highest level the civ reached (for Legends and the dump).
- `siteSim.tech map[string]int` is each civ's level **on this site as the
  history walks**. Every event with a civ snapshots it into
  `Event.Vars["tech"]`, so evidence and text render the level the civ had
  *then* (a castle from its medieval age, a bunker from its atomic age, on
  one site). No new event field.
- Pool civs are fixed at spacefaring. Natives roll a start from
  `civs.json` `native_tech` (`{"primitive": 60, "medieval": 40}`), capped by
  `species_tech_max` (`{"yeti": "medieval"}`: the yetis' ancestors never
  built reactors). Species mapping to today's creatures is unchanged.

#### Data

- `Requires.TechMin`, `Requires.TechMax` (the acting civ's level on the
  site).
- `Effects.Tech`: `"+1"`, `"-1"`, `"-2"`, or a level name. Regression is how
  a collapse turns a people into a remnant.
- New `tech_advances` event (native resident, `repeatable`, figure
  `inventor`), added as optional repeat stages to native arcs.
- **`data/history/tech.json`**, one entry per level:

```json
{ "id": "medieval",
  "structures": { "seat": "castle", "fort": "fort", "dwelling": "village",
                  "shrine": "temple", "works": "mine", "grave": "graveyard",
                  "battlefield": "battlefield" },
  "weapons": ["pikes", "crossbows", "siege engines"],
  "relics": ["old_steel_blade", "carved_idol"],
  "leaves": ["ruins", "graves"] }
```

  Evidence names a role instead of a script: `"script": "@fort"`. `Evidence`
  resolves it through the event's `tech` var. A role with no structure at a
  level drops the stamp (the datapad stays). Template fills `{weapon}` come
  from the same table.

#### Gating today's events

| Event | Gate |
|---|---|
| `reactor_built`, `nuclear_war`, `reactor_meltdown` | `tech_min: atomic` |
| `lab_founded` | `tech_min: industrial` |
| `observatory_built`, `archive_sealed` | `tech_min: medieval` |
| `civ_rises` | any; stamp becomes `@seat` |
| `civ_collapses` | any; `tech: "-1"`; stamp `@seat` with `damage: 3` |
| ships (`ancient_wreck`, `crew_crash`, …) | already cast pool civs (spacefaring) |

Native arcs that need a reactor (`extinct_radiation`) gain `tech_advances`
repeat stages before `reactor_built`; `TestLoreEveryListedArcReachesPresent`
catches an arc that can no longer finish.

#### What the player recovers

`tech.json` `relics` lists loot blueprints per level, placed by structure
scripts in containers through a new `fill_container(x, y, z, table)` builtin
(tables in `data/history/loot.json`). Primitive and medieval give trade
goods and old weapons. Industrial gives machine parts. Atomic gives
`radioactive_ore` and fission cores. Spacefaring gives alien alloy and a
`tech_fragment` that recipes in `data/research.json` can take as an input, so
a relic becomes research through the physical-storage path that already
exists. `civ_relic` history quests (`data/quest_templates.json`) name the
relic by level.

### 4. The two-civ war arc and its variants

#### A second civ slot

`siteSim` gains `rival string` beside `resident`. New civ roles:

| Role | Effect |
|---|---|
| `rival_native` | invents a second native civ on the site (same name rules, own species roll, own tech) |
| `rival_visitor` | a pool civ occupies the site without settling (an overlord) |
| `rivals` | casts `{civ}` = resident, `{enemy}` = rival |
| `rival` | casts the rival alone |

`Effects.RivalFate` mirrors `ResidentFate`. `Requires.Rival *bool` mirrors
`Resident`. Outcomes and `deriveFates` already handle any number of civs per
site.

**Per-side events.** `"per_side": true` makes `apply` emit the event twice,
once led by each side, each with its own figures and its own origin. Origins
of a per-side event roll in that side's half of the map. The halves are
split by an axis rolled once when the rival appears and stored on the sim:
`origin: "side"`. `origin: "border"` rolls in the middle band. The two
emitted events are linked through `Causes`, so the chronicle and the Legends
tab can show "meanwhile, across the river…".

**Each side's version.** A per-side event may carry `text_side` in place of
`text` for its datapad: the side's own account, with `{civ}` as "we".
`LoreDatapadFixtures` gains a perspective (the leading civ), and the datapad
at each fort reads that side's story of the same war. The chronicle keeps
the neutral `text`.

#### Arc `sundered_world`

Maps: earth_like, frozen_planet, alien (life remains), and desert_planet,
moon (its atomic ending leaves no life).

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | epoch | `native_life_emerges` | — |
| 2 | | `twin_peoples_rise` (`native` + `rival_native`, per side) | `@dwelling` at each side; figure `founder` ×2 |
| 3 | optional, repeat 1–3 | `tech_advances` (per side, independent rolls) | figure `inventor` |

can end the history: 3–5 of the survivors' species near their dwelling. They
can only be met as hostiles until the enemy factions plan gives natives a

### 5. A procedural structure library

#### The param contract

Every lore stamp receives, beyond its JSON params, what `Evidence` adds:

| Param | From |
|---|---|
| `event` | the event ID (for datapads and named rooms) |
| `civ`, `species`, `tech` | the acting civ and its level then |
| `purpose` | the event's `purpose` var (rolled from `vars.purpose`) |
| `damage` | 0 intact … 3 ruin; event param, raised by later events at the same origin (a collapse ruins the castle its rise built) |
| `size` | `small`/`medium`/`large`, chosen by map size class and budget |
| `occupants` | `none`, `guards`, `crew_alive`, `crew_dead`, `cryo`, `dormant`, set by tier and event |
| `holds` | a loot table ID from `loot.json` |

Any string param may use `{var}` fills from the event's vars. `@role`
scripts resolve through `tech.json`. Quest fixtures and scenario stamps pass
none of these, and every script defaults them to today's behaviour.

#### Places built for reasons you discover

`data/history/purposes.json` holds one entry per purpose
(`weapons_research`, `plague_study`, `xenobiology`, `terraforming`,
`forced_labour`, `prison`, `archive`, `observatory`, `shrine`,
`colony_hold`, `flagship`, …) with:

- **room names** a script attaches with `tag_region` (new hover text in the
  build: "Isolation Ward", "Specimen Tanks"), up to 3 per structure;
- **props and loot** per room (a `holds` table);
- **a clue**: a datapad placed in the innermost named room by a new builtin
  `spawn_lore_datapad(x, y, z)`, which binds to the `event` param. Its text
  is the purpose's clue line rendered with the event's fills ("…the
  specimens are not to be fed after the third bell…"). Picking it up reveals
  the event, the same as origin datapads do today.

So a `lab_founded` with `purpose: plague_study` is the same underground_bunker
shape as one with `xenobiology`, but its rooms, loot and clue differ, and a
later `lab_leak` reads true.

### 6. Playability rules

#### The landing reserve

Before any history evidence is placed, `BuildWorld` picks a **landing
reserve** with the plaza search, seeded from the site seed (not the global
`rand`), and tags it as region `landing_zone` (a 21×21 box).
`findStartingPlaza` tries `landing_zone` first, after the primer tags. Every
evidence feature has a keep-out from that box: 16 tiles for inert ruins, the
hazard's `keep_out` (default 40) for hazards and anything that spawns
hostiles. An origin rolled inside the keep-out is pushed out along the line
from the reserve during `ResolveOrigins`. Stations keep `station_hub` and
put the reserve there.

#### The budget

`data/generation.json` gains `lore_budget`, per map size class (small
≤180 wide, medium ≤384, large):

| Limit | small | medium | large |
|---|---|---|---|
| stamps (a mothership counts 3) | 3 | 5 | 7 |
| stamp footprint, share of surface | 3% | 3% | 3% |
| underground structures (shafts, dungeons) | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| hostiles spawned by lore evidence | 6 | 10 | 14 |
| of which from the hazard | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| radiation pockets from lore | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| min spacing between stamps | 20 | 24 | 32 |

Enforced in two places:

- **History side** (`History.Evidence`): each evidence block declares a cost
  (`stamps`, `hostiles`; defaulted from the script's `occupants` and the
  kind). Over budget, the oldest events' stamps drop first. The chapter's and
  hazard's evidence always stay, then the most recent remains. A dropped stamp
  keeps its datapad. This is deterministic and cheap, so tests can sweep it.
- **Build side** (`placeStamp`): stamps reserve their footprint plus spacing
  on the level; later stamps score overlapping candidates as unusable and
  avoid tiles of ore-bearing veins (`ore_vein` tiles count as not open).

Resources: evidence must leave at least 90% of the map's ore tiles reachable
compared with the same seed built without history. Mazes: at most the
underground limit above, and surface stamps keep at least one 2-tile gap per
side (the castle's gate already does).

## Open questions (defaults chosen)

- **Tier shares per map.** Default 35/45/20 everywhere. Station could run
  hotter (45/45/10) and asteroid calmer (25/45/30). Tune with `-stats`.
- **Hazard under a threat.** Default no: one chapter per site. A threat
  site can still show older hazards' evidence (fallout from a past war) but
  not a second live spawn.
- **Alien worlds without mutants.** Default: the alien base becomes calm,
  and mutants come from `mutant_band` (hazard) and the alien_world threats.
  If mutants are the alien world's identity, keep `alien_world` as base and
  accept fewer calm alien sites.
- **Remnant natives.** Default: hostile at low counts (`remnant_band`
  hazard) until the enemy factions plan gives natives a neutral stance. A
  friendly native blueprint is out of scope.
- **Natives reaching space.** Default no: a native civ caps at atomic.
  Later, a native civ that reaches spacefaring could join the pool and appear
  elsewhere.
- **Tier visible before charting.** Default: the tag shows from charting,
  like the summary.
- **Mothership on medium maps.** Default large maps only.

## Risks: the three riskiest assumptions

1. **The tier roll hits its shares.** Eligibility (requires, `origin_from`,
   repeats, safe start) varies per history, and fallbacks push weight down a
   tier. Maps with few eligible hazards (ocean, station) may land at 30% hazard
   / 35% quiet. Mitigation: `TestLoreTierShares` per map, enough hazards per
   map with no `origin_from` to fall back on.
2. **Footprints are honest.** The budget, spacing and landing reserve assume
   a structure stays inside its `w`×`h`. Underground scripts dig shafts and
   dungeons below the footprint, `circle_tower` stacks floors, and a broken
   ship's offset aft section and furrow reach outside it. Mitigation: the
   per-script footprint test, and a `bounds` declaration per script used by
   the budget.
3. **BASIC can carry procedural ships.** A hull generator with sections
   composed through `gen_structure`, a damage pass and named regions is far
   larger than any script today (the biggest, `underground_dungeon`, is 380
   lines). Interpreter speed on a 60×60 mothership field and nested
   `gen_structure` depth are unmeasured. Mitigation: build `crashed_ship` with
   scout and freighter first and time it in the structure viewer. If it is
   too slow, move the hull outline to a Go helper builtin and keep sections in
   BASIC.
