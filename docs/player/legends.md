# Legends

Every world in this region has a past: alien peoples who settled it, built on
it, fought over it, fled it or died on it. The **Legends** tab of the dashboard
(**O** or the Star Map button) is the chronicle of everything your expedition
has learned about that past, and of what your colonists have done since they
arrived.

## How you learn the story

Nothing is listed until you discover it:

- **Datapads.** Wherever something happened on a world, a datapad lies at the
  spot. A colonist who picks one up reads it out.
- **Getting there.** A colonist who reaches the place where something happened
  (an old wreck, a reactor, where a plague began) finds out what it was.
- **Ship scans.** A scan that charts a new world reads its story.
- **Quests.** Completing a quest tells you the history behind it.

Your expedition's own story is written in as it happens: colonies founded and
abandoned, colonists who died and who killed them, bosses slain, quests and
contracts, threats wiped out.

So is what the other [factions](factions.md) do: nests growing and firing
eggs, ships landing and lifting off, signals sent and machines waking, a
faction turning hostile, ships boarded and taken. What happens where you
cannot see is kept back until you find the faction it belongs to.

## The tab

The left list filters the chronicle: **All events**, or one **site**, one
**civilization** or one **figure** (a named person). The right side is the
chronicle itself, oldest first. It opens on the newest events; scroll up (the
mouse wheel or the scrollbar) to go back in time. The filter list scrolls the
same way once it is long. Prehistory is dated in years before landfall;
everything since is dated in the [star date](star_map.md).

## Why it matters

- The **Star Map** describes each world by its history, and says what is
  going on there now.
- A world's threat is the latest chapter of its story and grows from where it
  started: the nest from the wreck it came out of, the plague from where the
  dead fell.
- Some quests come from the history: recover a named engineer's log, the
  records of a vanished people, or find where a world's trouble began.
